There are thousands of AI tools and most of them are thin layers over the same handful of models. That makes choosing harder, not easier. This checklist cuts through the noise in about fifteen minutes per tool.

## 1. Name the job, not the category

"I need an AI writing tool" is too vague to evaluate anything. "I need to turn a 40-minute customer call into a one-page summary" is a job. Write down the specific task, how often you do it and what a good result looks like. Every later step depends on this.

## 2. Check whether a general assistant already does it

Before paying for a specialist tool, try the task in a general-purpose assistant such as [ChatGPT](/tools/chatgpt/), [Claude](/tools/claude/) or [Gemini](/tools/gemini/). Specialist tools earn their price through workflow, integrations and team features. If you only need the output, the general tool is often enough.

## 3. Read the data policy

Find out three things: whether your inputs are used to train models, how long data is stored, and whether you can delete it. For anything involving customer data, contracts or source code, this is a requirement, not a nice-to-have.

## 4. Understand what the pricing actually meters

AI pricing is rarely a flat fee. Look for the unit being counted: credits, characters, minutes, tasks or premium requests. Then estimate your monthly usage in that unit. Many tools look cheap until you hit the cap in week two.

## 5. Test with your own material

Vendor demos use inputs chosen to look good. Use your own messy document, your own codebase or your own recording. Run the same input through two competing tools and compare how much editing each result needs.

## 6. Check the exit

Can you export your work in a standard format? If the tool stores your content, prompts or trained voices, find out what happens to them when you cancel.

## 7. Decide what "worth it" means

Put a number on it. If a $20 subscription saves you two hours a month, that is an easy yes. If you cannot say what it saves, cancel after the trial and see whether you miss it.
