Most disappointing AI output traces back to a prompt that left too much to guesswork. You do not need special syntax or secret phrases. You need to brief the model the way you would brief a capable new colleague.

## 1. Give the context you would give a person

A model knows nothing about your situation unless you say it. Compare:

> Write a product description for our water bottle.

with:

> Write a 60-word product description for a 750 ml insulated steel water bottle. The audience is office workers who commute by bike. Tone: practical, no hype. Mention that it fits standard bike cages.

The second prompt takes twenty seconds longer to write and saves several rounds of revision.

## 2. Show an example

If you have a sample of what good looks like, paste it in. One example of your preferred tone or format communicates more than a paragraph of adjectives.

## 3. Specify the output format

Say whether you want a table, a bulleted list, three options or a single paragraph. Give a length. If the result will go into a spreadsheet or another tool, describe the structure exactly.

## 4. State constraints as positives

"Use plain words a twelve-year-old would know" works better than "don't be complicated". Tell the model what to do, and add the few hard limits that matter, such as a word count or a banned claim.

## 5. Iterate in the same conversation

Treat the first answer as a draft. Reply with specific feedback: "shorter", "the second point is wrong because…", "keep the structure but make it more formal". Assistants such as [Claude](/tools/claude/) and [ChatGPT](/tools/chatgpt/) hold the earlier context, so each round gets closer.

## 6. Verify anything factual

Language models can produce confident errors. For facts, figures and quotes, ask for sources or use a tool that cites them, such as [Perplexity](/tools/perplexity/), and check the source yourself.

## A reusable template

> **Role and goal:** what you are trying to achieve
> **Context:** audience, background, constraints
> **Input:** the text or data to work with
> **Output:** format, length and tone
> **Example:** a sample of the style you want

Fill in what applies and skip the rest. Not sure which assistant to practise with? See [ChatGPT vs Claude](/compare/chatgpt-vs-claude/).
